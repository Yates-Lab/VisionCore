**Extraretinal pathway effects in the full FIXRSVP condition.** **A**, native-bin responses for CID 98 in session Allen_2022-04-06. The four maps show observed native-bin rates, the uncalibrated full-model rate, and the separate gain- and additive-pathway rate effects. Observed rates are raw spike counts divided by the 240-Hz bin width (4.167 ms), so one spike in a bin corresponds to 240 spikes/s; this display conversion does not smooth the counts or infer within-bin spike times. The observed and full-model maps share a linear grayscale scale capped at 500 spikes/s. Higher rates take the darkest shade (500+ on the colorbar), while the underlying counts and predicted rates remain unchanged. The gain and additive maps share a signed-effect colorbar. Each map uses the same trial-relative time bins. Small hollow green right-pointing triangles mark all detected saccade onsets displayed in these trials, regardless of whether the detections qualify for the peri-saccadic summaries. The 56 chronological trials reached 0.8 s after their first recorded bins. The displayed common-support crop spans 0.2458–0.8000 s; unsupported bins remain masked. The signed maps share a unit-specific scale. Each pathway effect is its rate with that pathway separately enabled minus the rate with behavioral input set to zero. Because rates are obtained through a softplus nonlinearity, the two effects need not sum to the full-model change.

**B**, observed and full-model stimulus-aligned peri-stimulus time histograms (PSTHs) from precisely the trials and shared valid native bins in A. Observed counts and raw expected model counts were converted to spikes/s at 240 Hz, averaged across supported trials at each trial-relative bin, and smoothed over five native bins (20.83 ms) with support-weighted edge padding. Missing bins contribute neither counts nor denominator and remain unplotted when their center has no support. Bands show the mean ± one sample standard deviation across the 56 displayed trials after each trial's counts or predicted rate was smoothed separately over its supported bins. These bands show trial spread, not confidence intervals: the observed band includes spike-count noise, whereas the full-model band shows variability in predicted rates without simulated spikes. A band is undefined where fewer than two trials have supported bins. The plotted lower bound is the algebraic mean minus one standard deviation and can be negative even though rates cannot. No scaling or baseline correction was applied; the mean raw predicted/observed rate ratio over supported map bins is 1.280. The cached Figure 3 CCnorm of 0.862 (CCmax 0.937), used to rank this example on its separate scoring support, was not recomputed from B.

**C–D**, separate gain and additive rate effects aligned to qualifying saccade onsets for the same unit. Traces are means over all 50 wholly supported events, including events outside the A crop. Each complete event was smoothed over five bins before averaging. Bands show 2.5–97.5 percentiles of 400 bootstrap resamples of trials, retaining all events from each sampled trial. The display spans the full −100 to +200 ms around the detected event, and C and D use the same zero-centered vertical range encompassing both traces and bootstrap bands; the cached event windows and bootstrap traces are unchanged. This horizontal coordinate is event time rather than trial time.

**E, G**, gain and additive effects for 773 eligible units from 15 contributing sessions, using the same unit rows in both maps and a common symmetric color limit of ±9.156 spikes/s (the pooled 98th percentile of absolute effects). The empirical cohort required firing rates above 2 spikes/s and split-half PSTH R² above 0.10, with at least ten qualifying units per session; population inclusion did not depend on CCnorm. Of 1,022 eligible units across 19 processed sessions, 249 lacked at least 20 wholly supported event windows and were excluded. Rows are ordered by the lag of the positive gain maximum in 0–200 ms, latest at the top and earliest below; the 16 units without a positive gain peak occupy the bottom rows without a separator. Vertical lines mark saccade onset at zero. The maps display the full stored −100 to +200 ms population rows.

**F, H**, signed balance (horizontal) and total modulation (vertical) for every population unit (773 points per plot). For each existing five-bin-smoothed row, enhancing area P integrates max(effect, 0), and suppressive area N integrates max(−effect, 0), over inclusive native lag centers from 0 to 200 ms. Trapezoidal integration uses time in seconds. Because each row is an event-averaged rate effect, the resulting areas are in spikes/saccade; integration adds no smoothing, baseline subtraction, filtering or row normalization. Signed balance (P−N)/(P+N) ranges from −1 (negative only) through 0 (equal areas) to +1 (positive only). Total modulation P+N is in spikes/saccade. Balance is undefined at zero total area (none of the 773 units). The vertical reference marks equal areas. Both scatter plots use the same dimensionless horizontal range and shared linear vertical limits; they do not equate axis units. The small histograms below the scatters show the fraction of 773 units in each of ten common, equal-width signed-balance bins from −1 to +1, with +1 included in the last bin and the same linear fraction scale. Histograms to the right show the fraction of 773 units in each 0.05-spikes/saccade total-modulation bin, starting at zero and extending through the largest observed total; both pathways use identical bin edges and linear fraction limits. Median signed balance is −0.040 for gain and −0.784 for additive; median total modulation is 0.325 and 0.073 spikes/saccade, respectively. The additive balances concentrate toward both ends, with more near the negative end. These descriptive measures do not specify temporal order, peak count, statistically significant individual biphasicity or a tested population-bimodality hypothesis. These model-pathway counterfactuals do not measure biological gain or offset. Because the behavioral basis is acausal, event-aligned timing is descriptive and does not establish a causal neural latency.
